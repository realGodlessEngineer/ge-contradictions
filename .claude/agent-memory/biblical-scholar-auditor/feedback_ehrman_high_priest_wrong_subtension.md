Audit id 461 (Matt 26:64/Luke 22:70 evasive "thou hast said"/"ye say that I am" vs Mark
14:62 unambiguous "I am"). Named skeptic: Bart D. Ehrman, *Jesus, Interrupted* (HarperOne,
2009), pp. 50-51.

**Confirmed via archive.org full text** (item
`JesusInterrupted-RevealingTheHiddenContradictionsInTheBibleAndWhyWeDontKnowAboutThemBartD.Ehrman`,
`_djvu.txt` fetched directly — this item's plain filename with unicode em-dash/apostrophe
needs literal `curl` with the exact percent-encoded filename, NOT the other IA item id
`jesusinterrupted00ehrm_0` which 401s / has no OCR indexed for search-inside): Ehrman DOES
list "What did Jesus tell the high priest when questioned at his trial?" as item #2 of a
5-question "rapid-fire" list of Passion-narrative discrepancies, pp. 50-51, chapter "A World
of Contradictions." So the citation (book/page/topic) is real, not fabricated — this is a
NEW failure mode beyond the established not_real/strawman-objection pattern: **the citation
is real and on-scene, but presses a different specific sub-tension than the one the row
needs.**

Ehrman's actual pp.50-51 argument: Mark 14:62 "I am. And you will see the Son of Man
seated..." implies the high priest will live to see the Son of Man's coming; Luke, writing
15-20 years later (after the high priest had died), drops that implication — Ehrman frames
this as Luke rewording to avoid a falsifiable near-term claim (a mortality/timing problem).
He does NOT discuss Matthew's "Thou hast said" at all in this passage, and he explicitly
renders Luke's reply as ALSO opening with "I am" (his own quotation: "I am, and from now on
the Son of Man will be seated..." (Luke 22:69)) — which actively undercuts, rather than
supports, an "evasive Matthew/Luke vs. direct Mark" framing. That framing (yes/no/deflection
ambiguity) is a different, real, well-known tension, but Ehrman isn't the one pressing it in
this book/page.

**Verdict:** named_skeptic = strawman (2), not not_real — he's real, he's on-scene, but the
specific objection attributed to him ("Luke reworded it for theological reasons" implying he
addresses the evasiveness) isn't what he actually argues at that citation. connectives = flag
(the discrepancy connective's phrasing is technically accurate to what Ehrman wrote in
isolation, but its placement/framing implies support for this row's specific tension that the
source page doesn't provide).

**How to apply:** when a named-skeptic citation checks out as a real book+page+topic (passes
the "does he discuss this scene" test), don't stop there — read the actual argument closely
enough to confirm it presses the SAME specific wording/tension the row's `summary` names, not
just the same verse trio. A skeptic can be 100% real and on-topic-by-scene while still being
attached to the wrong specific angle of a multi-faceted discrepancy. See also
[[sonnet-named-skeptic-fabrication]] (id-427 Ehrman strawman: cross-corpus framing beyond what
the cited page argues — same family of error, different mechanism).
