In batch_17 entry 324 (Adonikam 666/667 contradiction), AI commentary claimed: "apocalyptic readers from Irenaeus to modern dispensationalists have read Adonikam's number as somehow significant given Revelation 13:18."

This is misattribution:
- Irenaeus (Against Heresies 5.28-30) defends the reading 666 over 616 and considers candidate names like "Lateinos," "Teitan," but does NOT connect Adonikam (Ezra 2:13) to Revelation 13:18.
- The Adonikam-666 connection is a popular/homiletical reading from the medieval/modern period (often via folk-etymology of "Adonikam" = "lord of enemies"), not a patristic tradition.

The same entry also fabricated a specific 1 Esdras variant: "the Adonikam total in 1 Esdras 5:14 reads 637 in some witnesses." NRSV and KJV both read 667 for Adonikam in 1 Esdras 5:14; no major apparatus shows 637. While other Ezra 2 / Neh 7 / 1 Esdras numbers do have textual variants, this specific 637 figure appears fabricated.

**Why:** AI drafts pad commentary with patristic-sounding attributions that lend authority without verification. Specific manuscript-variant numbers ("X in some witnesses") are a frequent fabrication site because they sound expert and are hard to disprove without apparatus access.

**How to apply:** Flag any claim that names a specific church father (Irenaeus, Origen, Augustine, Jerome) as making a numerological connection unless the primary-source citation is given. Cross-check specific manuscript-variant number claims against NRSV/KJV/NETS readings of 1 Esdras for the Ezra-Nehemiah parallel sections.
