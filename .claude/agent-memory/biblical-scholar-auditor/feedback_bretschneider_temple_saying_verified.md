id 460 (Did Jesus say "destroy this temple... in three days I will raise it up"? — John
2:19-21 vs Matt 26:59-61/Mark 14:57-58 false-witness trial testimony): the discrepancy
pole's named skeptic is Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider, *Probabilia de Evangelii et
Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine* (1820). The connective claims: "since
the synoptists themselves call the witnesses false, Jesus never spoke John's words at
all." This is **verified real and accurate**, not a strawman.

Direct confirmation source: D. F. Strauss, *The Life of Jesus Critically Examined*
(Eng. tr. George Eliot, 1846 4th ed.), Part II Ch. 1 §114 "Figurative discourses in
which Jesus is supposed to have announced his resurrection" (archive.org
`lifeofjesuscriti00straiala`, ~p. 578-579 of the scan): "the author of the Probabilia
appeals to the fact that the synoptists call the witnesses, who allege before the
judgment seat that Jesus had uttered that declaration, ψευδομάρτυρας, false witnesses;
whence he concludes, that Jesus never said what John here attributes to him" — with a
footnote citing "Probab., p. 23 ff." Strauss himself then argues against
Bretschneider's inference (a witness being called "false" doesn't necessarily mean the
underlying saying is invented rather than garbled), which is itself confirmation this
was a live 19th-century critical debate, not a fabricated one.

**Why:** Bretschneider recurs across this corpus (see [[feedback_paine_age_of_reason_vs_letters]]
and other named-19th-century-critic entries) as a named skeptic on Johannine-vs-Synoptic
discrepancies — his whole Probabilia project is a catalog of exactly this kind of
argument (John's Gospel diverges from/is unsupported by the Synoptics -> evidence
against apostolic authorship). Bretschneider mentions are real when tied to a genuine
John-vs-Synoptic tension; his Probabilia does NOT appear to be digitized on archive.org
directly (searched, not found), so verification routes through secondary quotation —
Strauss and Tholuck's *Commentary on the Gospel of St. John* (`commentaryongosp00thol`)
both quote/cite him extensively and by page.

**How to apply:** If Bretschneider reappears as a named_skeptic on a John-vs-Synoptics
row, use the archive.org fulltext search-inside technique
([[feedback_archive_org_fulltext_search_technique]]) against Strauss's *Life of Jesus*
(`lifeofjesuscriti00straiala`) and/or Tholuck's John commentary
(`commentaryongosp00thol`) first — both quote Bretschneider by name and page far more
reliably than a direct web search for the Latin Probabilia, which returns only generic
biographical results.
