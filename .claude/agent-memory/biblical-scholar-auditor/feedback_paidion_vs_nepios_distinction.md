---
name: paidion vs nepios Synoptic-Pauline lexical distinction
description: AI drafts collapse paidion (Synoptic kingdom-child saying) and nepios (Pauline immaturity / Matt 11:25 "babes") into single root claim
type: feedback
---

The Synoptic kingdom-child sayings (Matt 18:3, Mark 10:13-15, Luke 18:15-17, Matt 19:14, Mark 10:14, Luke 18:16) use **paidion/paidia** (παιδίον/παιδία). Paul in 1 Cor 13:11, 1 Cor 14:20, Eph 4:14, and Heb 5:13 uses **nēpios** (νήπιος). The two words have different lexical roots and different ranges of meaning:
- *paidion* = "little child" (Greek diminutive of *pais*, ~age 7 or under)
- *nēpios* = "infant" or "without speech" (also figurative for "immature/ignorant")

The one place Jesus is recorded using *nēpios* is Matt 11:25 / Luke 10:21: "thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes (*nēpioi*)." That is **NOT** the kingdom-child saying — different pericope, different argument (hidden revelation, not status reversal).

**Why:** AI drafts of contradiction commentaries on "is it good to be childish?" routinely claim Paul's *nēpios* is "the same Greek root the gospel tradition uses for the little ones Jesus blesses." That conflates two distinct words. The commentary can still make the larger point about overlapping child-imagery, but the lexical claim has to be qualified.

**How to apply:** In any audit of Matt 18:3 / Mark 10:15 / 1 Cor 13:11 / 1 Cor 14:20 / Eph 4:14 / Heb 5:13-14 contradictions or pairings, verify the specific Greek word being invoked. Synoptic kingdom-child = *paidion*; Pauline immaturity-imagery = *nēpios*; Matt 11:25 revelation-to-babes = *nēpios* (different argument).
