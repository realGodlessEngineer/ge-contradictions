id 471 ("Did both thieves revile Jesus?", Mark 15:32/Matt 27:44 both-mock vs
Luke 23:39-43 one-mocks-one-converts): the discrepancy connective read "Bart
Ehrman presses the same clash: Mark and Matthew show both criminals mocking
Jesus, while Luke gives one a wholly different, penitent role." Verified via
archive.org fulltext of *Jesus, Interrupted* (HarperOne 2009) that Ehrman's
cited pp. 64-69 are a section explicitly titled "An Opening Illustration: The
Death of Jesus in Mark and Luke" — he contrasts only Mark ("Both of the
robbers being crucified with him mock him," p. 66) against Luke ("explicitly
not by both men being crucified with him... one of them mocks Jesus but the
other rebukes," p. 68). Matthew is never invoked in this section at all.

**Why:** the underlying tension (both-mock vs one-converts) is real and
Ehrman genuinely presses it — so `named_skeptic` stays `ok` (he's real,
engaged this passage, fairly represented on the core contrast). But the
connective's parenthetical addition of "Matthew" to what Ehrman argues is an
unverified/false scope claim — a distinct failure from inventing a critic or
strawmanning the objection itself. This is the same *class* of error as the
id-470 precedent (connective overclaims beyond what the citation supports)
but manifests as added scope/attribution rather than added strength of
claim.

**How to apply:** when a connective names which Gospels/books a skeptic
"shows" or "presses," verify the skeptic's own chapter/section boundaries
(section headings are a strong signal) match that book list exactly — don't
assume the connective's book list is accurate just because the underlying
tension and the cited author are both real. Mark `connectives=flag` (not
`named_skeptic=strawman`) when the skeptic is real/fairly represented on
substance but the connective's claimed scope (which books, which specific
argument) overshoots the actual citation. See also
[[feedback_gill_prov24_verse_boundary]] for the parallel excerpt-metadata
(verse_ref) drift pattern in the same id.
