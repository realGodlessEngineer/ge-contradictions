- **Late-pass scholar parity is a recurring class**: pass-1 commentary edits routinely introduce new scholar names without matching scholarship entries; pass 2+ must actively re-scan for these, not just check the prior pass's flagged items (Pass-2 late scholar parity).
- **Editor-credit sweeps generalize**: a pass-4 fix on one editor credit (e.g. Grabbe as "ed.") signals a systematic sweep is needed across ALL chapter-in-volume citations in the next pass (Pass-5 editor-credit sweep).
- **Passing-mention parity gaps persist late**: batch_24 pass 5 found Westcott/Crossan parity gaps — the "scholars like X and Y" phrasing pattern silently drops a foundational name even in late passes.
- **TDOT/dictionary entry-author misattribution**: the TDOT chemah entry is by Schunck, not Fabry — verify a dictionary ENTRY's specific author, not just the volume editor (pass-6 batch_19).
- **Carson EBC page range outside volume**: Carson's Matthew EBC rev. vol 9 = pp.23-670; pp.671+ belongs to the Mark section, not Carson — caught pass-6.
- **Summary-field factual drift**: late passes must deep-read summary PARENTHETICALS, not just the commentary body — a Levi/Ephraim "brothers' nephews" garbling survived passes 1-6 undetected.
- **Summary-commentary internal consistency**: a summary's "same Greek verb" claim was directly contradicted by the commentary's own krinō/anakrinō distinction — cross-check summary claims against the commentary body, not just against scripture.
- General rule: convergence to 0 errors is NOT guaranteed by early-pass cleanliness — scope-fit, parity, and pagination errors are disproportionately introduced or exposed in passes 5-10, so don't shorten the pass budget based on early clean rounds.
- **Overclaim/tone drift**: pass-8 batch_19 also softened an overclaiming Blomberg attribution (connective overstated his position) — tone/overclaim drift is a distinct failure class from parity, check both.
