D. F. Strauss (Life of Jesus Critically Examined, 1846 Eng tr, §129 "The Denial of
Peter," pp. 658-661) genuinely catalogues the four gospels' disagreement over who
challenged Peter (portress/damsel/several-at-fire/another-damsel/Malchus's-kinsman/
Galilean-dialect-accuser/bystanders) and argues that Paulus-style harmonization
(treating every named challenger across all four gospels as a distinct event) forces
6-9 total denials, contradicting the "thrice" prediction each evangelist reports.
Verified verbatim via archive.org djvu text (lifeofjesuscriti00straiala), lines
~41905-41946 ("Peter would have denied Jesus from 6 to 9 times").

John Haley (Examination of the Alleged Discrepancies, 1874) treats the same tension
at pp. 424-425 (archive.org id examinationof00hale; page-425 header confirmed via
_page_numbers.json, leafNum 441 -> printed page 425), arguing no single evangelist
records every accusation and Peter likely repeated denials to a shifting cast —
"accounts for the difficulty as to the persons who accosted him, and the places
where he was when the denials were uttered." Verbatim-quote-checked, verified real
and on-topic.
