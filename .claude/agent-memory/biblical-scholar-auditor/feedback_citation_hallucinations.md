AI-drafted scholarship sections in this project have shown a recurring pattern of **fabricated essay/chapter citations inside legitimate edited-volume titles** AND **wrong specific page numbers within real monographs**. The primary monograph or commentary is real; the claimed internal essay or page range may be invented.

**Why:** Models confidently confabulate plausible-sounding essay titles attributed to real scholars in real edited volumes, especially when a genuine book by the same scholar already provides the needed citation. They also confidently guess page numbers that sound plausible but correspond to front-matter or unrelated sections. Caught examples:
- batch_01 entry 12: a fake Knoppers essay "Greek Translation and Hebrew Text: The 'Chronicle' of Eusebius's Apparatus and the Textual History of 1 Chronicles 1" attributed to Knoppers & McConville, Reconsidering Israel and Judah (Eisenbrauns, 2000) — that edited volume is real but concerns the Deuteronomistic History, not Chronicles, and the essay does not exist.

Gen 1-Gen 2 relationship "zoom-in" discussion in Hamilton is actually around pp. 151-153, not 137-144. Softened all three Hamilton sub-page ranges to chapter/verse references on pass 4.

**How to apply:** 
1. When a scholarship block contains BOTH a scholar's commentary AND an additional essay by the same scholar in an edited volume, verify the edited volume's actual table of contents. Cross-check: does the edited volume's subject matter match the essay's claimed topic? If not, treat the essay as likely fabricated.
2. For page-range claims inside major commentaries, cross-check against the volume's actual pagination. If the cited page is in front-matter (e.g., pp. 1-20 in an ICC that starts commentary at p. 19), it's wrong.
3. When a page range cannot be verified with confidence, soften it rather than leaving a plausible-but-unverifiable number.
