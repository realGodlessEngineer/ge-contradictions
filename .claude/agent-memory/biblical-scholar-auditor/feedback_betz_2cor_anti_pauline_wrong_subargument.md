id 550 ("Is it OK to marry unbelievers?", 1 Cor 7:12-14 vs 2 Cor 6:14-17) named Hans
Dieter Betz, "2 Cor 6:14-7:1: An Anti-Pauline Fragment?" *JBL* 92 (1973): 88-108 as the
discrepancy-pole named_skeptic. The citation itself is **entirely real** — Betz's
article is the single most-cited treatment arguing 2 Cor 6:14-7:1 is a non-Pauline
(likely Qumran/Essene-adjacent, or Judaizing-opponent) interpolation.

**Why flagged (strawman, not not_real):** the connective claimed Betz's evidence for
non-Pauline authorship was that the passage's separatist demand is "theologically
incompatible with the same author's permissive stance on mixed marriage in 1
Corinthians 7." Extensive WebSearch across many angles (Fitzmyer 1961 CBQ precursor,
Furnish AB 32A excursus references, general lit-reviews of the interpolation debate,
Wikipedia, hapax-legomena scholarship citing Betz) consistently describes Betz's
actual case as resting on: (1) vocabulary/hapax legomena foreign to Paul, (2)
Qumran-parallel light/darkness/"Beliar" dualism, (3) the break in argumentative flow
between 6:13 and 7:2, (4) the passage reflecting the theology of Paul's Judaizing
opponents (Torah-observance/separatism) from Galatians/Antioch. **None** of the
corroborated descriptions mention 1 Corinthians 7 or marriage at all — the classic
"inconsistent with Paul's own other statements" counterpoint used in this literature
is 1 Cor 5:9-13 (don't shun immoral people of *this world*), not 1 Cor 7:12-16. It
would also be a little self-undermining for Betz's authorship argument to lean on a
marriage-specific reading of 6:14, since even harmonizers like Clarke (this row's own
reconcile-pole excerpt) deny 6:14 is primarily about marriage at all.

**How to apply:** this is the [[feedback_ehrman_high_priest_wrong_subtension]]
pattern (real cite, wrong angle → `strawman` not `not_real`) recurring outside
Ehrman. When a named_skeptic's *specific* sub-argument is unusually well-tailored to
the row's exact contradiction topic (here: marriage) rather than matching the
skeptic's actual documented thesis (here: authorship/interpolation on internal
grounds), be suspicious the pipeline grafted an invented sub-argument onto a real
scholar/real work. Verify the *shape* of the argument, not just that the citation
exists. Betz JBL 92 (1973): 88-108 is safe to cite for "argues 2 Cor 6:14-7:1 is a
non-Pauline/anti-Pauline interpolation" — not safe to cite for a 1 Cor 7
marriage-specific line of reasoning without a direct quote confirming it.
