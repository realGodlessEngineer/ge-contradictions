D. F. Strauss is a VERIFIED-REAL named skeptic for the centurion's-servant contradiction (Matthew 8 centurion comes in person vs. Luke 7 he sends Jewish elders then friends).

**Why:** When a harmonization-audit row names Strauss as the skeptic pressing this Synoptic divergence, it is NOT a fabrication. Confirmed against the primary text (Gutenberg ebook 64037, George Eliot tr.). Strauss treats it in **§98 "Cures at a distance"** (the section grouping the centurion's servant, the Syrophoenician's daughter, and John's nobleman's son). His exact words: "the most important difference is one which runs through the entire narrative, namely, that all which according to Matthew the centurion does in his own person, is in Luke done by messengers." He explicitly rejects the agency-maxim harmonization (*quod quis per alium facit*), quoting Storr that "scarcely any historian would so perseveringly carry that metonymy through an entire narrative," and concludes the three accounts should be "traced to a single root" (developmental/mythical, not three independent cures). So the objection is fairly represented, not a strawman.
