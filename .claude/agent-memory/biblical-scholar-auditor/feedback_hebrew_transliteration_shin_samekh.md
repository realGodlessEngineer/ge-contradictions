---
name: Hebrew transliteration must distinguish shin from samekh
description: AI drafts sometimes write "sallisim" or "sabbat" when Hebrew has shin (ש), not samekh (ס)
type: feedback
---

When transliterating Hebrew terms in parenthetical scholarly titles or commentary, distinguish shin (ש, "sh" sound) from samekh (ס, "s" sound).

**Why:** AI drafts have written "sallisim" for שָׁלִשִׁים (shalishim, "officers" — Na'aman VT 38 1988). The "ll" cluster is also wrong — the Hebrew has a single lamed. Correct transliterations: shalishim (anglicized), šālîšîm (academic with diacritics). Similar trap: shabbat (ש) not sabbat (ס); shema (ש) not sema. JSTOR/scholarly titles often use the academic form with macrons and dots, which AI drafts may mangle into all-s versions.

**How to apply:** When verifying scholarly titles containing Hebrew transliterations, check the original JSTOR/publisher metadata. Common shin-words in scholarly literature: shalishim, shemittah, shabbat, shofar, sheol, shema. If the citation uses a single "s" where shin is intended, that's a transliteration error worth correcting either to the anglicized "sh" form or the academic "š" form (preserve whichever the published title uses).
