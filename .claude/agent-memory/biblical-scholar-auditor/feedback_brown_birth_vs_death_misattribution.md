Raymond Brown's *The Birth of the Messiah* (1977; expanded 1993, Doubleday/ABRL) covers Matthew 1-2 and Luke 1-2 infancy narratives ONLY. *The Death of the Messiah* (1994, ABRL) covers passion narratives across all four gospels. Birth came FIRST chronologically; Death is the successor work. Birth does not cover resurrection narratives at all.

**Why:** Pass 2 of batch_24 caught entry 477 commentary with the broken phrase "Raymond Brown in *The Gospel of John* and his *Death of the Messiah* successor *Birth* analysis treat the Johannine 'still dark' as a theologically loaded detail." Three errors compounded: (1) Birth is predecessor not successor; (2) Birth covers no resurrection material; (3) Brown's actual "still dark" analysis is in his AB John commentary 29A (already cited in scholarship).

**How to apply:** Reject any AI-drafted phrase that calls Birth a "successor" or "sequel" to Death. Reject any AI attribution of resurrection/passion analysis to Birth (infancy-only scope). Brown's resurrection-narrative analysis is in AB John 29A (1970) and partly in Death Vol 2 (1994). If commentary needs another Brown source for resurrection, the AB John already cited usually covers it.
