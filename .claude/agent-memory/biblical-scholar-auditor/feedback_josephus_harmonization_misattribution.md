---
name: Josephus harmonization misattribution
description: AI-drafted commentary tends to overstate what Josephus actually says when citing him as the source of a harmonization
type: feedback
---

When commentary attributes a specific Biblical harmonization to Josephus, verify whether Josephus actually presents that harmonization explicitly versus merely records the underlying tradition.

**Why:** Josephus Ant. 7.243 is cited in batch_13 entry 247 as "presenting the harmonization" that Absalom's three sons died young (resolving 2 Sam 14:27 vs 18:18). In fact, Josephus only records the pillar tradition and Absalom's conditional motivation ("if his children should perish, his name would remain by that pillar"). He does not explicitly state the sons died. The "three sons died young" harmonization is a rabbinic tradition, not a Josephan claim. This was caught only at pass 5.

**How to apply:** When a citation attributes a specific harmonization or interpretive move to Josephus (or to Philo, or to other Jewish/Christian first-millennium sources), the actual primary text often turns out to record the underlying tradition without making the explicit harmonization claim. The harmonization itself typically emerges in later rabbinic or patristic commentary. Verify by checking the actual section of the primary text via lexundria.com, earlyjewishwritings.com, or penelope.uchicago.edu rather than trusting the attribution.
