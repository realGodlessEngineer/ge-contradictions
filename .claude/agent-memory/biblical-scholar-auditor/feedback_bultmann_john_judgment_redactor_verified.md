Rudolf Bultmann's *The Gospel of John: A Commentary* (Eng. trans. Beasley-Murray/Hoare/Riches,
Westminster 1971; German 1941) really does attribute John's futurist-judgment material
(e.g. John 5:28-29 "resurrection at the last day", and elsewhere 6:39-44, 12:48) to a
later "ecclesiastical redactor," while reading the evangelist's own theology as **realized
eschatology** — judgment/life already accomplished in one's present response to Jesus's
word (John 5:24-25 "the hour is coming, and now is"). This is textbook, widely attested
Bultmann (see also [[feedback_bultmann_antitheses_verified]] for a separate confirmed
Bultmann citation in this project). So a `named_skeptic` connective on a John
judge/not-judge or realized-vs-futurist-eschatology tension crediting Bultmann with the
redactor move is `ok` — real critic, real engagement, not a strawman.

**Haley anchor**: John Haley, *An Examination of the Alleged Discrepancies of the Bible*
(1874), p. 120 (OCR "120" precedes the "DISCREPANCIES OF THE BIBLE" running header),
carries a parallel-text table "To judge the world." / "Not to judge." citing John 5:22,27;
9:39; 3:17; 8:15; 12:47, resolved via the two senses of Greek *krino* (judge vs. condemn)
plus present-mission/second-coming staging: **"'I judge no man,' i.e. after your manner,
or else, during my present mission. At his second coming 'he will' in the ultimate and
highest sense, 'judge the world.'"** — verified verbatim via archive.org djvu.txt OCR
(item `examinationof00hale` OR `examinationofall00hale`; full-text `inside.php` search
returned no hits — OCR quality/whitespace defeats it, so fetch the whole `_djvu.txt` and
grep locally instead, per [[feedback_archive_org_fulltext_search_technique]]). This is a
solid, reusable pd_work anchor for any future John-judgment-cluster deeper_learning check.
