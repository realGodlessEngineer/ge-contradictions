When commentary prose says "found in [modern commentary] and earlier in Keil-Delitzsch" or "going back to Keil-Delitzsch", the Keil/Delitzsch position is paraphrased and per pass-5 parity rules requires a scholarship entry.

**Why:** Late-pass exhaustive parity sweeps surface Keil as a recurring un-cited "earlier in" co-reference because earlier passes treated 19th-century commentators as generic shorthand. They are not shorthand when a specific harmonizing position is attributed to them.

**How to apply:**
- Use the appropriate volume of the *Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament* series (T&T Clark, Edinburgh, 1864-1901, trans. James Martin).
- For Joshua/Judges/Ruth: Keil, C. F. *The Books of Joshua, Judges, and Ruth*. 1865.
- For 1-2 Samuel: Keil, C. F. *The Books of Samuel*. 1866.
- For Kings: Keil, C. F. *The Books of the Kings*. 1872.
- For Genesis/Pentateuch: Keil, C. F., and Franz Delitzsch. *The Pentateuch*. 1864.
- For Psalms/Proverbs/Job: Delitzsch authors most wisdom volumes.
- Note the entry as "representative 19th-century harmonization" with brief position description.

Sites where Keil-Delitzsch appears as un-cited harmonizer (batch_11 examples): entries 204 (Judg 1:8/21), 205 (Judg 1:19), 211 (1 Sam 1:1), 213 (1 Sam 7:1-2), 217 (1 Sam 7:13), 218 (1 Sam 14:14). Six instances in one 20-entry batch confirms this is a high-volume pattern, not a one-off.
