AI-drafted commentary tends to overstate what Josephus explicitly says when
citing him as a harmonization source — but also watch for over-correction that
strips real Josephan content he DOES attest.

- **Ant. 7.243 (Absalom's sons / 2 Sam 14:27 vs 18:18):** Josephus records the
  pillar tradition and Absalom's conditional motivation ("if his children
  should perish, his name would remain by that pillar") but does NOT explicitly
  state the sons died young — that harmonization is rabbinic, not Josephan.
- **Ant. 8.249 (Abijam's mother Maachah / 1 Kgs 15:2 vs 2 Chr 13:2 Uriel):**
  Josephus DOES say Maachah is Absalom's daughter by a woman named Tamar, and
  that she is Abijah's mother. He does NOT mention Uriel of Gibeah or attest
  the "granddaughter" reconstruction (Tamar = Absalom's daughter who married
  Uriel and bore Maachah) — that bridge is a Keil-Delitzsch/Archer inference
  found nowhere in Josephus.

**Why:** primary texts often record the underlying tradition without making the
specific harmonization claim later attributed to them; the harmonization itself
typically emerges in later rabbinic or patristic commentary or modern
apologetics. Equally, don't over-correct and deny what Josephus DOES say.

**How to apply:** verify any Josephus attribution against the actual section
(lexundria.com/j_aj, Whiston translation, earlyjewishwritings.com,
penelope.uchicago.edu) rather than trusting the citation. A commentary/pd_work
note that says "Josephus transmits no X-Y-Z link" can itself be correct and
clean — flag only if a draft re-asserts Josephus as evidence FOR a link he
never makes.
