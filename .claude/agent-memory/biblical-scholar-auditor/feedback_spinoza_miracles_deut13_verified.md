Baruch Spinoza (1632-1677), *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus*, ch. 6 "Of Miracles"
(1670), genuinely argues from Deuteronomy 13 that even false prophets can work
miracles/signs, so signs alone cannot certify a true divine mission — confirmed
against the primary text (Project Gutenberg #990, TTP Part II, paras. 47-48):
"miracles could be wrought even by false prophets; and... unless men are honestly
endowed with the true knowledge and love of God, they may be as easily led by
miracles to follow false gods as to follow the true God." This is a real,
on-topic citation whenever a row's discrepancy pole leans on the
"miracles-don't-prove-divine-authorization" tension (id 606 — "Are miracles proof
of divine mission?": Matt 11:2-5/John 3:2/Exod 14:31 evidentiary vs Exod 7:10-12
(Pharaoh's magicians)/Deut 13:1-3 (false-prophet sign test)/Luke 11:19 (Jewish
exorcists)).

**Why:** Spinoza is a major, frequently-invoked critic on precisely this
theological-political tension and could recur across other harmonization rows
touching miracle-as-credential arguments; verifying him once against primary text
saves a re-verification pass later.

**How to apply:** If a future row's named_skeptic is Spinoza citing Deut 13 (or
TTP ch. 6 generally) against miracles-as-proof, treat the citation as verified
real and accurately characterized — re-confirm only that the specific connective
wording doesn't overclaim beyond "false prophets can also work signs, so signs
alone don't certify divine mission."
