id 527 ("Was Mary Magdalene happy or sad when she saw the risen Jesus?" — Matt 28:1-9 joy/feet-embrace
vs John 20:11-15 weeping/non-recognition): all three sourced claims verified real and accurately
represented.

**Strauss named_skeptic** (discrepancy pole): *Life of Jesus Critically Examined* (1835; Eng. tr. 1846),
**§ 137 "First Tidings of the Resurrection"** (Gutenberg ed. 64037-h.htm, search "FIRST TIDINGS OF THE
RESURRECTION"). Strauss discusses the standard harmonist move of supposing "Mary Magdalene had
previously separated herself from the women of whom Matthew speaks" (i.e. her solitary John encounter
is distinct from the group's Matthew encounter), then refutes it on its own terms: "since Matthew has
besides Mary Magdalene only the other Mary, it would have been only one woman to whom Jesus appeared
on the way from the grave: whereas Matthew throughout speaks of several (ἀπήντησεν αὐταῖς)." This is
the exact "Mary Magdalene split from the group" harmonization + plural-wording objection the work
file's connective describes — confirmed on-topic and fairly represented, not strawmanned.

**Haley pd_work** (deeper_learning): *An Examination of the Alleged Discrepancies of the Bible* (1874),
item `examinationof00hale`, **pp. 328-330** (leaf 344-346, leaf−16 offset — see
[[feedback_archive_org_fulltext_search_technique]]), quoting "Robinson['s Harmony]" verbatim: "Mary
Magdalene ... leaves the sepulchre and the other women, and runs to the city ... On the way, Jesus meets
them, permits them to embrace his feet" (the Matthew joyful-group encounter) vs "Mary Magdalene, who had
again followed them to the sepulchre, remained standing and weeping before it ... Turning around, she
sees Jesus" (the John solitary-weeping encounter) — an exact match to the pd_work note's two-separate-
parties-of-women summary. Running headers "328 DISCREPANCIES OF THE BIBLE" / "HISTORICAL DISCREPANCIES.
329" / "330 DISCREPANCIES OF THE BIBLE." confirm the page range with zero arithmetic ambiguity.

John 20:11-18 as separate chronological events, addressing the emotional/recognition contrast "only in
