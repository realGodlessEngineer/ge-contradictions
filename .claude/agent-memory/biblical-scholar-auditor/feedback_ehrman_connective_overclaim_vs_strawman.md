Audit id 479 ("When did the women discover that Jesus's body was missing?" —
Luke 24:2-4/John 20:1-2 discovery-then-angel vs Matt 28:5-6/Mark 16:5-6
angel-then-discovery). Named skeptic on the discrepancy pole: Bart D. Ehrman,
*Jesus, Interrupted* (HarperOne, 2009), pp. 47-60.

**Confirmed via the book's own djvu full text** (archive.org item
`jesus-interrupted-bart-d.-ehrman`, `_djvu.txt` — the OTHER Ehrman IA item id
`jesusinterrupted00ehrm_0` has no search-inside index; use this one or the
unicode-filename item noted in [[ehrman-high-priest-wrong-subtension]]):
Ehrman's pp.48-49 "A World of Contradictions" chapter runs a long catalog of
rhetorical either/or questions about the resurrection narratives, and it DOES
include this row's exact item — "Had the stone already been rolled away from
the tomb (as in Mark 16:4) or was it rolled away by an angel while the women
were there (Matthew 28:2)? Whom or what did they see there?" — i.e., did the
women discover the tomb empty on their own, or was an angel already present
announcing it. So unlike id 461, the specific sub-tension the row needs IS
one Ehrman actually raises at the cited pages — this is not a wrong-scene or
wrong-angle substitution.

**The catch:** the row's connective claims Ehrman treats this AS "one of the
Gospels' clearest, unharmonizable contradictions" — but two paragraphs later,
in that same passage, Ehrman writes "One point in particular seems to be
irreconcilable," and reserves that specific superlative for a DIFFERENT item
(the Mark-silence vs. Matthew-immediate-Galilee-appearance discrepancy about
whether the women/disciples ever told anyone), not the discovery-order/angel
item. The discovery-order item is presented as just one bullet in an
enumerated "on virtually every detail they disagree" list, not singled out
as the clearest/most irreconcilable one.

**Verdict:** named_skeptic = ok (0) — Ehrman is real and the specific item is
genuinely his, fairly represented on substance. connectives = flag (1) — the
connective borrows a "clearest/irreconcilable" degree-claim from a different
point in the same source passage, which is a real but milder failure mode
than [[ehrman-high-priest-wrong-subtension]]'s strawman (there, the cited
pages didn't press the row's sub-tension AT ALL; here, they do, just without
the superlative the connective attaches to it).

**How to apply:** when auditing an Ehrman *Jesus, Interrupted* "rapid-fire
list" citation (chapter 3/"A World of Contradictions", the pp.47-60 range
recurs often in this corpus), (1) confirm the specific item is literally one
of his listed questions — real citations here are usually genuine because the
list is long and covers most classic resurrection/passion discrepancies —
then (2) separately check whether the connective's superlative/emphasis
language ("clearest", "irreconcilable", "most damning") actually belongs to
THIS item or was borrowed from Ehrman's one explicit "seems to be
irreconcilable" callout elsewhere in the same passage (which is about the
Galilee-appearance/silence discrepancy specifically). Item-real + degree-word
borrowed = connectives flag, not named_skeptic strawman/not_real.

**id 517** ("Was it OK to touch the risen Jesus before his ascension?" —
John 20:17 "touch me not" vs. Matt 28:9/Luke 24:39/John 20:26-27). Reconcile
pole's connective: "Commentators note Christ's body was soon handled by the
women and Thomas, and that the verb may mean 'cling not' rather than an
absolute ban." The sole accompanying excerpt is Gill on John 20:17 (verbatim,
verified) — Gill DOES make the "touched later by Thomas/women" point, but his
note never raises the Greek verb-tense ("cling"/present-imperative-middle)
argument at all. The "cling not" claim is real, well-attested scholarship
(NIV "do not hold on to me", ESV "do not cling to me"; also independently
confirmed on the row's own gotquestions.org/touch-Mary-Thomas.html deeper-
learning link, which explains the Greek as "cling to, lay hold of") — so it
is not fabricated, just not sourced by the one excerpt that follows the
connective. Verdict: connectives = flag (only the first half of a two-part
connective claim is grounded in the accompanying excerpt), while the excerpt
itself stays overall=pass (in_source/on_tension/pole_label all clean) and
named_skeptic on the discrepancy pole (Wells/SAB) is unaffected = ok. This is
the reconcile-pole analog of id 409 (logged in
[[feedback_harmonization_clean_log]]): a connective's compound claim
outrunning what a single accompanying excerpt actually shows, even though
every individual clause is independently true.

**How to apply (general form):** when a pole's connective makes more than
one factual claim, check EACH claim against the accompanying excerpt(s)/
named-skeptic text separately — a connective is not "accurate to what it
introduces" merely because every clause is true in the abstract; each clause
needs to be traceable to the specific quoted material that follows it. A
claim that's true-but-unsourced-here is still a connectives flag, not a
pass, even when there's no fabrication or strawmanning involved.
