The Smith-Cross paired-name parity rule (pass 2) extends to three-name authority lists when all three scholars are presented as jointly arguing a specific substantive thesis. Pattern: "X, Y, and Z have all shown/argued that [specific claim]".

**Why:** Discovered in batch_18 pass-3 entry 344 (Where does God dwell?), where commentary attributed a P/D divine-presence theological synthesis to "Israel Knohl, Mark Smith, and Stephen Geller" — Knohl and Smith had scholarship entries but Geller did not. Added Geller's *Sacred Enigmas* (Routledge 1996, pp. 62-86) to the array.

**How to apply:** Distinguish three patterns:
1. **Three-name authority for specific thesis** (parity required): "X, Y, and Z have argued that Israelite religion contained P/D tension" — all three need entries.
2. **"From X to Y" passing-range list** (no parity): "every commentator from Hossfeld and Zenger to Brueggemann to Dahood reads it this way" — passing reference, generic agreement, not parity-triggering.
3. **Generic apologist or skeptic list** (no parity): "Carson, Schreiner, Moo standardly read this as..." — list framing, not individual thesis attribution.

The key test: does the prose attribute a *specific* argumentative move to all named scholars jointly? If yes, parity. If it's "everyone agrees" or "the range of opinion runs from X to Y", no parity needed.
