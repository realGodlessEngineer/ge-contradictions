Augustine's actual harmonization of Mark's "third hour" (15:25) vs John's "sixth hour" (19:14) in *De Consensu Evangelistarum* III.13:

1. **Primary solution**: The Jews "crucified" Jesus by their voice/clamor at the third hour; the soldiers physically nailed him up at the sixth hour. Two-stage agency, not two clock systems.

2. **Alternative solution**: "Parasceue (preparation) reckoning" — sixth hour of parasceue ≈ third hour of day. Some scholars also note Augustine elsewhere flirted with a scribal-error explanation (Greek numeral γ vs ϛ confusion) but this is not his primary move in III.13.

The dual-time-reckoning hypothesis (John uses Roman civil time counting from midnight; Mark uses Jewish daylight reckoning counting from sunrise) is a **modern apologetic move**, typically attributed to B. F. Westcott in his *Gospel According to St. John* (Murray, 1881). Carson picks it up cautiously in PNTC John (1991). It is NOT Augustine's solution.

**Why:** AI drafts (and some popular apologetics like Geisler/Howe) wrongly compress "Augustine harmonized the discrepancy" + "the dual-reckoning solution exists" into "Augustine proposed dual-reckoning." This conflates two distinct harmonizations across 1500 years.

**How to apply:** Any commentary attributing the Roman-civil-time hypothesis to Augustine is misattribution. The proper attribution is Westcott (modern origin), with Carson and other evangelicals as later adopters. Augustine's own move is the Jewish-clamor/Roman-action two-stage agency. Verified via De Consensu Evangelistarum III.13 newadvent.org.
