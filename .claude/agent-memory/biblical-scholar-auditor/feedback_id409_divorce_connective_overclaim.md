**Flag found:** the reconcile connective claims Tyndale's editors "name all
four positions directly" — but the note only names **three**: Mark+Luke count
as *one* absolute-prohibition position, and Deuteronomy 24:1-2's permissive
"any uncleanness" reading (the summary's fourth pole) is **never mentioned**
in this Mark-10 note at all. This is a real connective overclaim (Guardrail
5) even though the excerpt itself is accurate and on-topic — a good example
of the E-line (excerpt) grading clean while the G-line (connective) still
flags.

**Why:** connectives that tally "all N positions/texts" are worth arithmetic-
checking against what the *summary* actually lists as the N poles, not just
against what the note happens to cite. A note can be a strong reconcile
excerpt while its accompanying connective still overcounts.

**How to apply:** when a connective asserts it covers "all N" viewpoints,
count the texts actually present in the excerpt against the summary's stated
list before accepting the claim. Also verified clean in this row: Wells/SAB
