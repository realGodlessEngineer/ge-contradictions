id 598 (Prov 11:31 earthly recompense vs Rev 20:12/Matt 16:27/2 Cor 5:10 eschatological
judgment) cited Bart D. Ehrman, *God's Problem: How the Bible Fails to Answer Our Most
Important Question—Why We Suffer* (HarperOne, 2008) as the named skeptic on the
discrepancy pole. Verified real and fairly represented via WebSearch: the book's central
structure contrasts a "classical view" (Deuteronomy + **Proverbs** share the worldview
that obedience brings blessing, disobedience brings suffering/punishment **in this life**
— explicitly named as sharing this worldview) against an "apocalyptic view" (Daniel,
Jesus, Paul, Revelation — evil currently reigns, but God will soon intervene; vindication/
reward/punishment **shifts from the present to the future, from this life to the
afterlife**). Ehrman argues these are competing, not-reconciled biblical answers to
suffering, not phases of one plan — matches a connective phrased that way exactly.

**Why:** This is a different Ehrman book from the ones already catalogued in this
memory dir (*Jesus Interrupted*, *Misquoting Jesus* territory — Synoptic/textual
issues). *God's Problem* is his theodicy book and is the natural citation for any
this-world-vs-eschaton or "why do the righteous suffer" contradiction — good source
to expect and verify quickly rather than treat as suspicious on sight.

**How to apply:** When a discrepancy-pole named skeptic is Ehrman + *God's Problem*
on a reward/suffering/judgment-timing tension, the classical-retribution-vs-apocalyptic
-deferral framing is his genuine, well-documented thesis — confirm via WebSearch for
the specific verse pairing rather than assuming fabrication. Also see [[feedback_ehrman_haley_tomb_angels]]
and [[feedback_ehrman_connective_overclaim_vs_strawman]] for the general Ehrman-verification
posture (real book, but always check the connective doesn't overclaim beyond his actual
argument).
