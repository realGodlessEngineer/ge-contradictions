In Passion/Resurrection narrative batches (batch_24), commentary prose frequently names scholars with phrases like "articulated by B. F. Westcott in his Gospel of John commentary" or "from scholars like Crossan and Norman Petersen" — and these passing mentions get included in scholarship lists for the secondary scholar (Petersen) but not the lead-named one (Crossan), or for the modern apologist (Carson) but not the historical scholar (Westcott).

**Why:** Pass 5 of batch_24 caught:
- Entry 472 (crucifixion hour): commentary names B. F. Westcott as articulating the dual-reckoning hypothesis. Scholarship listed Carson PNTC John (who *picked up* Westcott's hypothesis) but not Westcott himself. Added Westcott, Gospel According to St. John (John Murray 1881).
- Entry 480 (women's silence Mark 16:8): commentary says "from scholars like Crossan and Norman Petersen." Scholarship listed Petersen (Interpretation 1980) but not Crossan. Added Crossan, Birth of Christianity (1998).

**How to apply:** In late-pass parity sweeps for Passion/Resurrection batches, scan commentary prose for two-name pairs ("X and Y") where one is a modern scholar with discrete article-style citation and the other is a foundational/historical scholar. The article-citation scholar is usually present in scholarship; the foundational scholar is the one most often dropped. Especially watch for: Westcott (paired with Carson on dual-reckoning), Crossan (paired with Petersen on Mark 16 ending or Brown on burial), Bultmann (paired with Brown on Johannine theology). Trajectory note: batch_24 went 5→2→4→2→2 — non-monotonic, recommend pass 6.
