For contradictions turning on the Luke 7:36-50 sinful-woman vs. John 12:1-8 Mary-of-Bethany
identity question (e.g. id 505, "Who washed Jesus's feet with her tears/hair"), the
Jamieson-Fausset-Brown note on Luke 7:37 — "There is no ground whatever for the popular
notion that this woman was Mary Magdalene, nor do we know what her name was" (cross-ref'd
to Luk 8:2) — is a **real, verbatim** JFB quote, but it rebuts only the **Magdalene**
composite-identification tradition. Checked JFB's Luke 8:2 note too (via blueletterbible.org):
it also only distinguishes Mary Magdalene from the Luke 7 sinner ("It is a great wrong to
this honored woman to identify her with the once profligate woman of Luk 7:37, and to call
all such penitents Magdalenes") — **neither note ever mentions Mary of Bethany**.

**Why:** A TRANSFORM-pass connective/rationale that paraphrases this excerpt as "keeping her
distinct from John's Mary of Bethany" (rather than just "from Mary Magdalene") overclaims —
it borrows the excerpt's authority for a different, unaddressed identification question. This
is the same failure mode as the id-470 "dissolves the wording difference" case in
`AUDITOR_SPEC.md` guardrail 5: accurate quote, connective claims more than the quote shows.

**How to apply:** on_tension = weak (not full on_tension) and connectives = flag when a JFB
Magdalene-denial excerpt is being used to argue the Bethany question. This pattern will recur
across any id in the Mary-of-Bethany/Luke-sinner/Magdalene "composite Magdalene" cluster
(id 505 and likely siblings) — check the connective's noun phrase (which of the two/three
women is actually being denied) against the excerpt text every time.

D. F. Strauss's actual position on this cluster (verified, Gutenberg #64037, *Life of Jesus*
Eng. tr. 1846, Part II §89 "Narratives of the anointing of Jesus by a woman," pp. 402-409):
Luke and John's foot-drying-with-hair detail is "given by both in nearly the same words,"
and he concludes "we have here but one history under three various forms" (Matt/Mark + Luke
+ John collapsed into one). A connective compressing this to "one story, not two" for the
Luke-vs-John comparison specifically is a fair, non-strawmanned simplification — verified
clean (id 505 named_skeptic=ok).

Haley 1874 anointing-harmonization anchor (verified verbatim, archive.org
`examinationofall00hale` djvu, p. 411): "Christ anointed at one time, Matt. xxvi. 7; John
xii. 3; at another time, Luke vii. 37, 38 (the best critics hold that the anointing in the
first two passages was quite distinct from that mentioned by Luke)." Good deeper_learning
pd_work anchor for this whole cluster.
