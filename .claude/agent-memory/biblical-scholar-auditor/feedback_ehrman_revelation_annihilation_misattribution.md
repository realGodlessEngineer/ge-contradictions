Audit id 367 (Does hell exist? — four afterlife strands: apocalyptic/ECT, annihilationist,
universalist, Sheol-only), 2026-07-01/02. Discrepancy pole named_skeptic = Bart D. Ehrman,
*Heaven and Hell: A History of the Afterlife* (Simon & Schuster, 2020). This is a REAL work
that genuinely engages exactly this question (joins How Jesus Became God id-2 PASS and NT
Historical Introduction id-18 PASS as verified Ehrman works — see [[sonnet-named-skeptic-fabrication]]).

**The connective was a FOUR-CLAUSE compound**: "the Bible preserves genuinely incompatible
afterlife views: Sheol's finality, Daniel's resurrection, an annihilationist strand in Jesus
and Paul, and Revelation's eternal torment." Three clauses verified accurate against three
independent reviews (amateurexegete, Gospel Coalition, sharedveracity.net extended review) +
Ehrman's own blog. The FOURTH clause is wrong: Ehrman's signature argument in this book is that
Revelation, LIKE Jesus and Paul, teaches ANNIHILATION for humans (the "second death" in Rev
20:11-15 = permanent destruction, not conscious torment) — he reserves eternal conscious torment
ONLY for the devil/beast/false prophet (Rev 20:10), non-human supernatural entities. A hostile
Gospel Coalition review even states this explicitly: Ehrman argues "Jesus, Paul, and Revelation's
author all taught annihilationism." So "Revelation's eternal torment" as one of Ehrman's four
incompatible strands inverts his actual conclusion for that book.

**Verdict given:** named_skeptic = ok (0) — the person/work is real and genuinely on-topic, three
of four clauses check out, engagement is not fabricated. connectives = flag (1) — the specific
"Revelation's eternal torment" clause misstates what Ehrman argues. Did NOT downgrade to strawman,
since a strawman implies weakening/caricaturing the skeptic's objection for easy dismissal; this is
a factual misattribution of a sub-claim within an otherwise-genuine, otherwise-accurate compound
connective, not a caricature.

**Why:** distinct failure mode from [[sonnet-named-skeptic-fabrication]]'s not_real/strawman cases
(invented engagement or reassigned SPECIFIC objection). Here the skeptic AND the general engagement
are both genuine — the error is buried in one clause of a multi-clause summary connective describing
several of the skeptic's positions at once.

**How to apply:** when a connective lists MULTIPLE sub-claims attributed to one named skeptic (a
survey-style "X argues A, B, C, and D"), verify EACH clause independently via WebSearch/WebFetch —
do not stop at confirming the skeptic is real and the overall topic matches. A multi-clause
connective can be 75% accurate and still need a flag for the wrong 25%. Do not assume genre
consistency (e.g., "Revelation = the ECT book" folk assumption) — checked scholars sometimes argue
the opposite of the conventional reading, and getting that backwards is exactly the kind of
sub-claim error worth flagging.
