Choon-Leong Seow's Anchor Bible Ecclesiastes (AB 18C, 1997) dates Qohelet to the **Persian period** — roughly the second half of the 5th to first half of the 4th century BCE — on the basis of Persian loanwords, Aramaisms, and economic/commercial vocabulary. He is specifically known for arguing AGAINST the Hellenistic / third-century-BCE consensus and resists reading Qohelet as engaging Greek philosophy.

**Why:** batch_04 entry 366 commentary_out misattributed to Seow both "situates this within Hellenistic-era Jewish wisdom" and "(third century BCE on Seow's dating)" — the exact opposite of his published position. This is a scholar-position-inversion, the same error class as [[feedback_markus_barth_authorship_position]] and the multi-volume year/position drifts.

**How to apply:** when a draft attributes a *date* or *interpretive stance* to a named commentator, verify the scholar actually holds it — do not assume they hold the field's majority view. For Ecclesiastes dating specifically: Seow = Persian period (minority); majority/Longman/Krüger lean Hellenistic 3rd c. BCE. The Stoic/Hellenistic *thematic* comparison (anakyklōsis, cyclical time) is fine as a parallel but must not be hung on Seow's dating. AB 18C volume number is correct; Eccl 1:9 Hebrew = "ein kol chadash tachat ha-shemesh" (הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ, sun) — distinct from Jer 10:2 "otot ha-shamayim" (הַשָּׁמַיִם, heavens).
