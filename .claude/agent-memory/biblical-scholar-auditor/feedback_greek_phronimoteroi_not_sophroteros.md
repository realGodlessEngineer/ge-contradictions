The Greek word for "wiser" in Luke 16:8 (parable of the unjust steward) is φρονιμώτεροι (phronimōteroi, plural masc. comparative of φρόνιμος / phronimos = "prudent, shrewd").

NOT σωφρότερος (sophroteros, from σώφρων / sophron = "self-controlled, sober"). These are two distinct Greek adjectives with different roots and meanings.

Pattern: AI conflates them because both translate as "wise/prudent" in some English glosses, but the lexical fields are distinct:
- φρόνιμος = practical wisdom, prudence, shrewdness (related to phronesis)
- σώφρων = moral sobriety, self-control, temperance (related to sophrosune)

**Why:** Caught in batch_26 entry 508 audit (2026-05-18). AI draft asserted "Jesus' 'wiser' in 16:8 is sophroteros in the Greek" which was wrong. Verified via BibleHub Greek text analysis (Strong's 5429 = phronimos comparative).

**How to apply:** Any time AI drafts cite Greek for "wisdom" terms, verify the specific lexeme. Greek wisdom terminology has at least four distinct roots: phronesis (φρόνησις), sophia (σοφία), sophrosune (σωφροσύνη), and gnosis (γνῶσις) — they're not interchangeable. This extends to the broader Greek-form hallucination class.
