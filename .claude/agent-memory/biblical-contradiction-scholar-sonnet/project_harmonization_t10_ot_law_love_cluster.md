Companion to [[project_harmonization_t10_run]]. Six-id leg on OT-law/NT-love-command style rows
(not the census/genealogy family). Results:

- **173** (Ammonites: Deut 2:19 protection vs Jephthah/Jeremiah 49 destruction) — full parity.
  Colenso's *Pentateuch Examined* Part I (colenso-pentateuch-examined.txt, §596-597, lines
  ~3607-3652) directly juxtaposes the D.ii.19 protection clause against Jer.xlix.1,4 (Ammon still
  independent/judged in Jeremiah's day) — a genuine on-point textual-critical engagement, not a
  stretch. This file has heavy OCR noise on quoted Bible text specifically ("Hatii" for "Hath",
  "thot" for "thou", "Bather" for "Rather") — copied verbatim per the no-normalization rule, but
  picked a segment ("Hatii Israel no sons?...") that was the least garbled available.
- **144** (love/hate brother: Lev 19:17 vs Luke 14:26, parityTarget 2) — only 1 of 2 sourced.
  Remsburg's *The Christ* (§566, lines ~11024-11032) quotes Luke 14:26 verbatim and calls it an
  "accursed teaching" causing "sorrows and miseries" — solid discrepancy-pole engagement. A second
  independent PD critic pressing this SAME pair could not be found (checked Strauss, Voltaire,
  Paine, Ingersoll's two works) — Ingersoll's rejoinder text with "He cannot love God and hate his
  brother" turned out to be Judge BLACK's words being rebutted, not Ingersoll's own (a trap: this
  pamphlet interleaves both correspondents — check voice before using a quote from it). Authored
  with 1 quote rather than force a weak second; flagged the parityTarget shortfall as partial.
- **149** (God loves everyone: Malachi 1:3/Esau vs 1 John 4:8, parityTarget 3) — only 1 of 3
  sourced. Paine's *Age of Reason* Part I ("word of a demon, than the Word of God" — the OT's
  "unrelenting vindictiveness") is a strong, famous, on-point passage for the general "vindictive
  OT deity vs loving God" charge, though it doesn't name Esau/Malachi specifically. Note: this
  Gutenberg full-text has an editorial footnote bracket `[NOTE: ... —Editor.]` interrupting Paine's
  own sentence mid-clause ("half the Bible [NOTE: ...] is filled") — had to splice across it with a
  single … ellipsis and grep-verify each side independently (the footnote is NOT Paine's words).
  Could not find 2 more independent critics on this exact Esau/hatred-of-nations theme despite
  checking Ingersoll, Voltaire, Spinoza pt.1 (which discusses fear-of-Lord/wisdom, not God-hatred).
  Authored with 1 quote, flagged shortfall.
- **137** (Lev 4:13-21 one-bullock vs Num 15:24-26 bullock+goat sin offering) — honest-absence.
  No well critic (checked Colenso vols/examined, Paine, Ingersoll) engages this specific
  sacrifice-count discrepancy; only tangential "young bullock" hits (Troki quoting Job, Wellhausen
  on priest ordination) that don't compare Lev 4 to Num 15.
- **150** (eunuch: Deut 23:1 exclusion vs Isaiah 56:5 inclusion) — honest-absence. Renan's *The
  Apostles* discusses the Acts 8 Ethiopian eunuch at length (church-admission precedent) but never
  engages the Deut/Isaiah tension specifically; Colenso/Voltaire/Ingersoll hits on "eunuch" are all
  off-topic (Potiphar, Saul's court, divorce-teaching analogy). Not a fit.
- **152** (fear God: Deut 6:13 etc. vs 1 John 4:18 "perfect love casts out fear") — honest-absence.
  Spinoza pt.1 discusses "fear of the Lord" as wisdom/true-worship (Proverbs 2) but affirmatively,
  not as a discrepancy; no well critic presses the fear-vs-love-casts-out-fear tension at all.

General gotcha reconfirmed: multi-voice PD pamphlets (Ingersoll's *Christian Religion* rejoinder to
Judge Black) interleave the OPPONENT's original text with the critic's rebuttal — always read
enough surrounding context to confirm WHOSE words you're about to quote before using a "hit".
