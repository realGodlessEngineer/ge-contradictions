When a Paine connective on a Genesis 1:31-vs-Genesis 6:6-7 ("Was God satisfied with his creation?") row paraphrases the argument that God is "a changeable, passionate, vindictive Being; making a world and then drowning it, afterwards repenting of what he had done," cite **Letters Concerning The Age of Reason (1797)** — specifically Paine's letter "An Answer to a Friend," Paris, May 12, 1797.

**Why:** The auditor's G-line on id 33 flagged this as a work/year misattribution and directed re-citing to *The Age of Reason* Part I (1794). That is a confirmed FALSE POSITIVE. Primary sources (thomaspaine.org "Age of Reason Letters" / deism.com) place the exact drowning-then-repenting-vs-creation's-unchangeableness contrast in the May 12, 1797 letter. Part I (1794) argues God's unchangeableness only as a general "word of God" epistemology argument — it does NOT contain the Flood/Genesis-6 repentance contrast this specific tension needs. Re-citing to 1794 would have broken a correct citation.
